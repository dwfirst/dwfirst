### `HOW I WORK WITH MODELS`

I don't write prompts. I describe a system the way I would describe a piece of music: a metaphor first, then a shape, then the boundaries — and I often leave the boundaries open on purpose.

Two independent AI agents that have worked with me on code described the same pattern from different angles:

> *"You give a metaphor and a goal, not a specification — 'make it like Kid in Knight Rider' instead of 'add an indicator with a timeout'."*

> *"You rewrite the task from the other side: not 'fix it', but 'why did this happen' — and you often find the problem before I do."*

Both are true. I work in two modes:

- **Generative mode** — fast, metaphorical, underdefined. I describe what the system should *feel* like, and I trust the model to fill the gaps. This is where ideas are born.
- **Diagnostic mode** — slow, precise, causal. When something breaks, I stop describing and start asking "why". This is where ideas are tested.

The weakness is real: I sometimes skip the plan check before saying "do it". The strength is also real: I hold a concept across more than three prompts, when most people lose the idea by the third.

I define what a system should become. I am honest about where my specification ends and implementation begins. And I test ideas against real tasks, not against my own expectations.
